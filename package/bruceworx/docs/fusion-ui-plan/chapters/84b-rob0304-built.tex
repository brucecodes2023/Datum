Two commits (ROB-03, ROB-04), all Python. The maths is FreeCAD-free in \file{addons/BruceWorx/bruceworx/balance.py}
(21 unit tests in \file{tests/test\_balance.py}); the assembly walk and the balance inputs are in
\file{bruceworx\_massreport.py}; the two panels, the CG marker and the commands in \file{bruceworx\_massreport\_gui.py}.
All your answers D1--D5 were kept.

\paragraph{What you can do now.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Typed masses.} \code{MeasuredMass} and \code{DatasheetMass} are in the part-property schema (the
        lowest layer, \file{part\_properties.json}). The part's mass is the measured one, else the datasheet one,
        else a body's \code{MassOverride}, else volume $\times$ density; the part's computed \code{Mass} follows the
        same order. With a typed mass the centre of mass stays the shape's centroid and the inertia is the shape's
        scaled to that mass (split by volume over the part's bodies).
  \item \textbf{Mass Report} (Solid tab, Inspect, group \emph{Mass \& Balance}): walks the selected assembly (else the
        active one, else the document) through links, sub-assemblies, link arrays and routed wires. A table of item,
        quantity, mass source, mass and share, biggest first; the total, the CG, and the inertia about the CG and
        about the origin. \emph{Export CSV} writes the table plus the totals; \emph{Show CG} draws a red sphere and a
        plumb line through the CG on the model (it goes when the panel closes). Parts without a mass are listed in
        amber.
  \item \textbf{Drone Balance}: select a motor part and \emph{Mark selected as motor} (adds \code{ThrustAxis},
        default $+z$ in the part's frame, and \code{ThrustTable}), \emph{Attach thrust CSV} to the picked row (the file
        is read once to check it and its path is recorded on the motor part; keeping the file with the vault item
        comes later). Pack capacity is typed in the panel or read from \code{CapacitymAh} on the battery part. The
        panel shows T/W, hover throttle (where each motor gives $mg/n$), hover current, flight time $0.8\,C/I$, each
        motor's hover share, throttle and current, and the checks in green, amber and red. Thresholds are the
        preferences \code{Balance\_tw\_amber}, \code{\_tw\_red}, \code{\_offset\_amber}, \code{\_offset\_red},
        \code{\_hover\_amber}, \code{\_hover\_red} under \code{BaseApp/Preferences/BruceWorx} (defaults 2, 1.5, 2\,\%,
        5\,\%, 60\,\%, 80\,\%); there is no preferences page for them yet.
  \item \textbf{Thrust CSV}: columns by header (throttle, thrust, current, voltage, any order and extra words such as
        ``Thrust (g)''), or in that order without a header; throttle given as 0--1 becomes percent.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Decided while building.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item The frame plane is the plane perpendicular to the mean thrust axis, so a frame lying in any orientation works;
        the motor's point is the centre of the motor in the model, its axis is \code{ThrustAxis} rotated by the
        occurrence's global placement.
  \item With fewer than three motors, or motors in a line, the shares cannot trim a CG offset; the check turns red.
  \item \emph{Mass Properties} moved into the new group (the group's caption did not fit over two buttons in the
        narrow-window test).
  \item The Physical tab (MOD-11) still ignores the two new properties; the report and the part's \code{Mass} use them.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Tests.} \file{TestBruceWorxBalance} (16, with a GUI panel test) on the figure's model rebuilt with the same
dimensions: 388.1\,g and CG $(2.45, 0, -2.73)$\,mm reproduced; measured over datasheet over override; a typed mass
keeps the centroid and scales the inertia against the box formula; a link array inside a linked sub-assembly; the
density path; a routed wire's own mass; motors found with global axes. Also \file{test\_balance.py}
(shares sum to $mg$ and balance moments, symmetric quad against $\frac{mg}{4}+\frac{mg}{2a^2}\mathbf r_i\cdot\Delta\mathbf c$,
interpolation, flight time), \file{TestBruceWorxPhysical}, \file{TestBruceWorxWorkspaces}, \file{TestBruceWorxInspect}
and the lint \file{TestBruceWorx} pass.

\paragraph{Open question: the acceptance figure and the 2\,\% limit disagree.} The figure's CG is 2.45\,mm off the
thrust centre; the motor diagonal is 230\,mm, so that is 1.07\,\%, \emph{under} the 2\,\% amber limit of D4. The
offset check therefore reads ok for it by default (the front motors do carry 3\,\% more than the rear ones, shown in
the table); a 1\,\% limit or a battery 12\,mm forward flags it. The epic's wording was ``a fraction of the arm
length'': 2\,\% of the arm (115\,mm) is 2.3\,mm, which does flag it. Which should the default be?
\textbf{Recommendation:} measure against the arm length (half the diagonal), keeping 2\,\% and 5\,\%.
