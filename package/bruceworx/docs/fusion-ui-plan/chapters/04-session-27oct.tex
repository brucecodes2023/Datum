\section{Session of 27 October 2026: motion, routing, simulation}\label{sec:session-27oct}
%======================================================================

You asked for three things at once: ``motion joints in assemblies, wire routing for electrical wires and
pipes/hoses, and lets start working through the simulation module and interface'', with the roadmap updated as
the work goes and questions asked first. One question round settled the scope; the answers are below. Three
agents build in parallel while the plan sections are written, so each epic has a \emph{plan} subsection (the
design, the theory and the open questions) and a \emph{Built} subsection that the agent appends when it finishes.

\paragraph{What was found before asking.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Motion.} FreeCAD 1.1.4's Assembly already has 13 joint types, four of which are motion links
        (Gears, Belt, Rack and pinion, Screw), and a kinematic \emph{Simulation} (\code{Assembly\_CreateSimulation}):
        one Motion per joint with a formula in \code{time}, solved by the Ondsel solver, scrubbed with a slider.
        Keystone's ribbon showed six joint commands; the other seven and the simulation were in the Assemble
        group's \emph{more} list. What Fusion adds is the user-facing layer: the Motion Study timeline with
        keyframes, Drive Joints, Motion Links by ratio, contact sets.
  \item \textbf{Routing.} Nothing in the roadmap and nothing in FreeCAD core. Fusion has no routing; SolidWorks
        Routing (electrical, piping, tubing) is the parity target. The \emph{Cables} add-on (sargo-devel, LGPL v3,
        0.4.0 of 26 Oct 2026, FreeCAD 1.0+) models wires, connectors and bundles on BIM's Pipe and Draft's Wire,
        and calls itself alpha. Pipes and hoses have only the old Dodo/Flamingo tools.
  \item \textbf{Simulation.} CalculiX 2.23 is in the development environment (\file{pixi.toml}) but
        \textbf{not in Keystone.app}; Netgen is built in; gmsh is absent. The ribbon's Simulation workspace was the
        stock FEM toolbars. \eref{SIM-01}--\eref{SIM-05} had the theory of \S\ref{sec:sim} and no plan or code.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Your decisions (27 Oct).}
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lX@{}}
\toprule
Motion & \textbf{Kinematic first}: Motion Study timeline (keyframes per joint, play, loop, ping-pong, speed,
  frame export), Drive Joint, Motion Link by ratio, all 13 joint types on the ribbon, built on the existing
  kinematic Simulation. Dynamics (gravity, forces, contact) stays \eref{ASM-10}, planned after. Epic \eref{ROB-05};
  plan \S\ref{sec:motion-plan}.\\
Routing & \textbf{Our own Routing workspace}, not the Cables add-on: a route path through picked points with a
  bend-radius check, feeding wire, pipe and hose objects; lengths flow to the BOM; from-to list and cut list.
  SolidWorks-Routing scope with the Fusion-style UI. New track \textbf{ROU} (\eref{ROU-01}--\eref{ROU-04});
  plan \S\ref{sec:rou}.\\
Simulation & \textbf{Static stress end to end} first: study wizard, Netgen mesh, CalculiX bundled in Keystone,
  safety-factor field, report, cantilever benchmark. Modal, buckling and thermal follow on the same frame.
  Epic \eref{SIM-01}; plan \S\ref{sec:sim01-plan}.\\
Process & \textbf{Build while you review}: the plan sections are written in parallel with the agents, which
  start on the parts that need no decision. Your answers to the question tables steer the rest.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
